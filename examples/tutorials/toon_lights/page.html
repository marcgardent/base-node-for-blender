Toon lights

Toon shading as Blender 5.3 lets a material write it: light by light. Each light cuts its own hard band, sets its own threshold and can turn into a rim light, from a custom property on the light itself. The tutorial ends with the same picture in one call: the module toon of the free standard library.

Before you start

You need Blender 5.3, with EEVEE: the lighting nodes are new in 5.3, and Cycles does not run them. You also need the Base Node add-on; step 5 needs a version whose standard library has the module toon. Everything here is free.

Every block below is a complete .bn file, its code beside the nodes it builds. Two ways to try a step:

  • Drag it. Drag the file icon of its block, or its title, onto empty space in Blender's 3D Viewport, and keep Instantiate the Example: a new object carries still_life at the 3D cursor, with its material Toon. A click on the file icon downloads the file instead.
  • Paste it. Press the copy button of its block, open a Geometry Node editor, press Ctrl+Shift+V and pick Add as Modifier, on any mesh object.

The lights are not in the files: they belong to your scene. Each step says which ones to add. Set the viewport to Rendered to see the material at work. The pictures are rendered from these exact files by render.py.

What Blender 5.3 changes

Before 5.3, a toon material in EEVEE went through ShaderToRGB: it turned the light on the surface into a colour, and a colour ramp cut it into bands. That light is the sum of all the lights. Two lights make one blurred sum, and nothing tells you which light lit what.

Blender 5.3 adds a node, LightAccumulation. What feeds its Light inputs runs once for each light, then Blender adds the lights up. Upstream of it, a handful of nodes see the light being evaluated:

NodeWhat it gives, for the light being evaluated
LightEvaluationfactor: how much the surface faces the light, 0 to about 1; mask: its falloff; direction, distance.
ShadowRaycastWhite where nothing casts a shadow from this light, black where something does.
LightInfoThe light's color, power and position.
Attribute[LIGHT]A custom property of the light object.

Three rules: these nodes only reach the output through a Light input of LightAccumulation, never through its Color inputs, and never through ShaderToRGB. LightAccumulation renders the lights and nothing else: no world light, no ambient.

1. One band per light

Add a sun, from the front left, pale warm. Then the file:

{{code step1_band.bn Toon band still_life}}
  1. The band. factor * mask says how much the surface faces this light. MapRange from threshold to threshold + 0.02 turns it into 0 or 1, with an edge two hundredths wide: the hard line of a cel.
  2. The light's colour. LightInfo().color * band(): where the band is 1, the light adds its own colour.
  3. The surface's colour. still_life stores a colour on each object, the attribute color, and the material reads it as the Diffuse Color: one material for the four objects.

2. A shadow of a colour

Same sun. Two changes in the material:

{{code step2_shadow.bn shading band Toon still_life}}
  1. Cast shadows. ShadowRaycast is the shadow this light casts on the surface. Multiplied into the band, the shadow of the ring on the floor is cut as hard as the rest.
  2. The shadow tone. Where no light reaches, the surface is its colour times shadow, a dull blue, added by an Emission: it does not depend on any light.
  3. Tunable. The material takes no input, so the shading goes in a graph of its own, shading: its shadow is an input of its node in the material.

Try: set shadow to (0.5, 0.3, 0.45, 1.0) on the node shading: a dusk mauve.

3. The light sets its band

Add a second sun, from the left, blue, and give it a custom property: select the light, Object Properties, Custom Properties, New, name it toon_threshold, a Float, value 0.6. Leave the first sun as it is.

{{code step3_threshold.bn light_threshold shading band Toon still_life}}
  1. Read on the light. Attribute[LIGHT] reads toon_threshold on the light being evaluated: the blue sun cuts its band at 0.6, a narrower band than the warm sun's.
  2. A light without it. A property the light does not have reads with an alpha of 0. Mix by that alpha takes the fallback, 0.45, for the warm sun.

4. A rim light

Add a third sun, orange, almost level, from behind the objects on the right. Two custom properties on it: toon_threshold at 0.25 and toon_rim at 1.0.

{{code step4_rim.bn shading light_rim light_threshold band Toon still_life}}
  1. Its own colour. A light on the Diffuse Light input is multiplied by the surface's colour: orange on blue makes a dull green. On the Glossy Light input, whose colour is white, it keeps its own.
  2. Which input. toon_rim sends the light there: lit * (1.0 - rim) to the diffuse, lit * rim to the glossy. The two other suns have no toon_rim: 0.
  3. Only the edges. The sun comes from behind: it reaches the sides that face away from the camera, and the edges of the others. Nearly level, it barely touches the floor, under its threshold.

5. The module toon

The same three lights. Steps 1 to 4 are the module toon of the standard library, which reads the same custom properties, and toon_intensity too:

{{code step5_module.bn Toon still_life}}

The knobs

WhereNameWhat it does
node tooncolor, shadowThe surface's colour, and what it is multiplied by where no light reaches.
node toonthreshold, softnessWhere the band of a light without toon_threshold starts, and how wide its edge is.
node toonintensityHow strong a light without toon_intensity lights.
lighttoon_thresholdWhere this light's band starts, 0 to 1: higher is narrower.
lighttoon_intensityHow strong this light lights.
lighttoon_rim1: this light adds its own colour, as a rim. 0: it lights the surface's colour.
